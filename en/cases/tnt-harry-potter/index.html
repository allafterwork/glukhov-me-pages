Idea

The ad your competitors broadcast for you

When content is good enough, it spreads without payment — through media, other channels, ordinary people. That's exactly how TNT's Harry Potter viral worked: the staged battle between Dumbledore and Voldemort was so spectacular and unexpected that other TV channels aired it in their own broadcast, and news sites published it as a genuine story.

The promo became an event. That's what separates viral content from merely good content: it lives beyond the media plan and works in places nobody planned for.

Mechanics

A staged duel as a news story

The film is shot in the genre of a staged fight: Dumbledore versus Voldemort — with costumes, special effects and full immersion in the Harry Potter aesthetic. The format provokes a reaction: you want to share it, write about it, add it to a collection. That's exactly what happened — at scale and organically.

Result

Earned media instead of paid

The film appeared in the broadcast of several TV channels and in the feeds of almost all major news sites. This is what media planning calls earned media — reach you can't buy directly; you can only earn it with the quality of the content.

Context

TNT off-air marketing — going beyond the broadcast

The Dumbledore viral is part of TNT's broader off-air marketing strategy: creating content that works beyond the television broadcast and lives in the digital space by its own rules. Social media, news sites, YouTube — these are all additional screens where the channel's brand needs to be present with the same personality as in the programming grid.
