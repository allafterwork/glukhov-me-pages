Idea

What you can make with AliExpress

Most people perceive AliExpress as a platform for cheap goods. Arina Filimonova disproved this with a single project: from affordable components bought on the platform, she assembled a myosuit — a technology that serious engineering teams with multi-million budgets work to develop.

The documentary film doesn't sell a product. It shifts the angle: AliExpress isn't about cheap — it's about access. Access to components, to knowledge, to the ability to do things that once seemed out of reach.

Hero

Arina Filimonova, Russia

14 years old. Self-taught bioengineer. Built a myosuit — a device that reads electrical muscle signals and converts them into control commands. All parts ordered on AliExpress. No lab, no grants — just curiosity, persistence, and a platform with 160 million products.

Format

Documentary instead of advertising

No slogans, no call to buy. Just the story of a real person — filmed honestly, without manufactured pathos. That's why it works: the viewer sees themselves in Arina and thinks "what could I make?" The platform becomes a tool, not just a shop.

Why it works

The best ad is the one you don't want to skip

The myosuit is not a metaphor or CGI. Arina actually built it, and it actually works. When a story is true, the audience feels it without explanation. The film doesn't say "buy on AliExpress" — it shows what happens when you have access to it. The difference between these two approaches is the difference between advertising and content you want to watch to the end and share.
